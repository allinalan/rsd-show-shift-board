RSD Show Shift Board · visual direction

Almanac, Varsity, Instrument

Three ways the board could look, each applied to the same real weekend — 9/11, 21 open shifts, Sierra Vista and Navajo Nation. Same markup in all three, so what you're comparing is the identity, not the layout.

First, the thing that's been holding the design back

Every strong color on this board already means something. That's why the current version looks washed out — the chrome had nowhere left to go, so it defaulted to grey-blue.

green = open grey = full amber = needs action burnt orange = pending violet = Elite tier teal = Key tier near-black = Traditional

What's actually left to brand with: brass, gold, indigo, oxblood, and the ground itself. Each direction below picks one and commits — the accent never touches a status color, so a rep never has to wonder whether gold means something.

Almanac

A printed field guide to the Arizona show circuit. Warm stone ground, brass rules, a display face with some grain to it. Reads like something made by people who've worked these fairs for years, not software that got pointed at a spreadsheet.

Varsity

You already sort reps into Freshmen, JV, Varsity and Elite. That's team-sport language, so this direction stops pretending otherwise — deep navy, jersey gold, heavy uppercase display, and the open-shift count treated like a score on a board.

Palette

What it costs you
Dark-first is harder to read in a bright hall, and a 7-column grid on navy gets heavy. Best if you want reps to feel recruited.

Four things I'd fix no matter which you pick

  1. The board opens on history. Weekends of 8/28 and 9/4 sit above this weekend. A rep lands on two dead rows before seeing a single open shift — it should open on the next live weekend.
  2. The tally shouts the wrong number. "Full 68" is currently darker and louder than "Open 177". Open shifts are the only figure a rep can act on, so it leads and the rest step back.
  3. Open shifts are styled like code. The green day chips are the single most important thing on a card and they're set in mono at 11px. In all three directions above they're a real control.
  4. On a phone you scroll ~1,400px before an event appears. Masthead, tally, four filters, two chip rows and a legend paragraph come first. The filters should collapse behind one button until a rep asks for them.

Pick a letter and I'll build it into the live board — identity, the four fixes, and the motion pass — then we look at it on your laptop and your phone before any of it goes near GitHub.
